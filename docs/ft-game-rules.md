## E. Decision rules

**From the 22 Oct evening review the endgame stance in part D of the core doc overrides this part wherever they conflict** (approved by Tim, 21 Oct 22:38 UK). In short: no score floor or trend test for a new position, no downtrend replacement, no 10% caps on results, momentum or drift positions, no results budget (still printed and quoted), no volatility caps or theme limit; the exit rules, the gate, `order_expected`, confidence ratings and the crypto rules stay.

**Score.** Out of 100: trend 40, short term 20, catalyst 20, revisions 20. About 40 is neutral. The pipeline calculates it; never work it out by hand. Once a stock has 200 stored closes the 50-day and 200-day averages, RSI and the change over a year (or over as far back as the store goes) are worked out from those closes; until then the published figures are used. The script's AVERAGES line says which stocks are still on published figures.

**Strength bands.** The pipeline gives every stock a band tied to the rules below, and the page colours the watchlist by it. Strong (green): a score of 60 or more with no "below 50-day average" or "downtrend" flag, so it can be bought as a core position. Weak (red): a score under 45 or a "downtrend" flag. Middling (amber): everything else. A strong stock can still be flagged "extended", "overbought" or "faded gap". "Extended" and "faded gap" limit size and timing as written below; "overbought" is a warning only.

**Moving averages.** The script also gives 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200-day simple averages, 10, 20, 50 and 200-day exponential averages, whether the exponential ones are stacked in order (`ema_stack`: up, down or mixed) and any 50-day and 200-day cross in the last 20 sessions (a "golden cross" or "death cross" flag). They are context for the review and are shown on the page. They do not change the score, so the score stays comparable through the game; the panel test and the calibration log are what decide whether the score should change.

- A new core position needs a score of 60 or more and no "downtrend" or "below 50-day average" flag.
- A holding that scores below 45, or carries the "downtrend" flag, is replaced at the next open unless a dated catalyst inside five trading days justifies keeping it. If kept, say why and cap it at 5%.
- A stock flagged "extended" is not added at more than 5% until it has pulled back or consolidated.
- "Overbought" (a high RSI) is a warning, not a cap: strongly trending stocks can stay overbought for weeks. Name the flag in the review when it is on a stock being bought, and size by the ordinary rules. Whether overbought stocks go on to lag is checked from the shadow ratings, not assumed.
- A strong single day says nothing about the trend. RTX and Lockheed Martin were bought on 8 Oct after one strong day inside a decline; do not repeat that.

The two judgement inputs, each 0 to 20 with a one-line note:

- `catalyst_pts`. 16 to 20: confirmed results or another dated event inside the next 10 trading days with a supportive lead-in (peers beating, estimates rising, strong pre-announced numbers). 11 to 15: a dated catalyst inside 10 trading days with a neutral lead-in, or a theme tailwind with fresh confirming news this week. 6 to 10: a theme tailwind with no dated event, or an event more than 10 trading days away. 0 to 5: no catalyst, or a clear overhang.
- `revisions_pts`. 16 to 20: an upgrade from a major bank in the last 7 days, or three or more target raises in the last three weeks. 11 to 15: one or two target or estimate raises in the last two weeks. 8: nothing notable. 0 to 5: downgrades or target cuts dominate.

**Confidence ratings.** Every position in an order, every watchlist promotion and every exit carries a rating from 1 to 100 (for an exit, how strongly the review expects it to lag). Three different things carry numbers here, and they are never mixed up (19 Oct evening, after Tim's review brief):

- **Model score** (0 to 100, frozen 40/20/20/20): a relative ranking of stocks. The confidence anchor (below) is the score rescaled, so it adds nothing to it.
- **Confidence (not calibrated)**: the review's forward-looking judgement, anchored to the score and moved at most 5 points. It is not a probability. On 19 Oct the settled record was 11 order ratings from two dates and 47 shadow ratings from two overlapping windows; the shadow anchor's Brier score equalled that of always saying 50, and its rank correlation with the excess return was about zero on both dates. Telling a true 60% from 50% needs about 190 independent forecasts, and about six non-overlapping five-session windows remain in the game, so the ratings cannot be calibrated before 27 Nov.
- **Observed calibration**: the share of comparable settled ratings that beat the index, always quoted with the count and the number of distinct dates behind it (for example "8 of 11, from 2 dates").
- Wording, everywhere Tim reads it: say "confidence 60 (not calibrated)", never "a 60% chance", "60% probability" or "likely to beat" on the strength of a rating. A rating may rank one candidate above another; it is never quoted as the odds of a result. This stays until Tim agrees, with evidence, that it can change.
- Label. Every order table headed for Tim calls the column "Confidence (not calibrated)" (part I). Reaching 150 settled shadow ratings does not lift the label: ratings made on the same few dates share one market move, so a count of ratings is not evidence of calibration.
- Start from the pipeline's confidence anchor (50 + 0.35 x (score - 40), held between 40 and 68, minus 3 in a risk-off regime). Move at most 5 points either side with a stated reason.
- Honest ratings sit between about 45 and 65. Anything above 65 needs a specific stated reason. Never inflate a rating to sound decisive.
- A holding rated below 50 needs a reason to stay (for example balance against another position) or should be replaced.
- Settlement (pipeline version 7.3): over the first five index sessions from the fill date, from the stock's traded open on the fill date to its close on the fifth of those sessions, against the index over the same sessions. A missing row, or an open that was never supplied, makes the rating wait; after 15 sessions it settles as "n/a" with the reason, counting as neither a win nor a loss. Shadow ratings likewise need the stock's close on the exact end date.
- The anchor is a starting assumption, and the panel test (below) has so far found no evidence that the price part of the score ranks stocks. Until it does, treat a high score as a filter (trend intact, not broken) rather than as an edge, keep ratings close to 50 to 60, and say so when a rating leans on the score alone.

**News momentum.** Strong news can carry a stock for several sessions, and that continuation can be captured even though orders fill at the open.

- Qualifying events: a rating upgrade or large target increase from a major bank (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, UBS, Barclays, Deutsche Bank and similar); a material contract, financing or government deal; a guidance raise or a clear beat-and-raise (for results, the earnings-drift scan below applies first).
- Separate the gap (open versus previous close) from the follow-through (open to close). Only moves from the fill onwards can be captured; the gap on the day the news broke cannot.
- Enter at 5% to 10% only after follow-through: the stock closed above its open, in the upper half of the day's range, on volume well above its recent average, and the news changes earnings, funding or the analyst case. Cap at 5% while it is below its 50-day average.
- A stock that gapped up and then closed below its open has shown a faded gap. Keep it on the watchlist and require a second session of strength.
- Exit at the next open after the first session in which it closes below its open on heavy volume, or gives back more than half of the previous session's gain, or after about five sessions without a new high. These moves end abruptly: never above 10%, never average up. Halve the size or skip in a risk-off regime.
- Worked examples (Vistra, Palantir, RTX and Lockheed Martin, Goldman Sachs, Meta) are in section 10 of the state doc.

**Earnings drift.** Results season runs to mid November. A company that beats and raises guidance tends to keep drifting for days after the first gap, and that drift is capturable here because an order fills at the next open. Each evening review scans the day's reporters (procedure in H1 step 4, data in part F) and passes them to the script as `drift` entries. The script runs six tests on the first full session after the report and gives a verdict; it never decides from a headline alone.

- The six tests: **beat** (earnings above the consensus forecast and revenue at or above it); **raised** (full-year or next-quarter guidance raised; "reaffirmed", "lowered" or no guidance fails); **reaction** (up 3% or more on the day and at least 2 points ahead of the S&P 500); **held** (closed at or above its open, in the upper half of the day's range); **volume** (at least 1.5 times its 20-day average); **trend** (above its 50-day average and the 50-day not below the 200-day).
- Verdict **enter**: all six pass. The stock may join the order at the next open at up to 10%, or 5% if it is more than 15% above its 50-day average (the script prints the limit), within every guardrail in part D: volatility cap, theme limit, the results budget for anything else reporting, and a confidence rating like any other position. Give it a score and judgement points like any new stock (G3), and start its `note` with "Drift position:".
- Verdict **watch**: beat, reaction and trend pass but one of raised, held or volume fails. Add it to the watchlist with status "Drift: needs 2nd day". It may be bought only after a second session that closes above its open, in the upper half of its range, and only at 5%. A beat without a raise stays a watch at best: Goldman Sachs on 13 Oct (a 31% earnings beat, no raise, faded from its open, below its 50-day) is the case to remember.
- Verdict **fail**: no action. It is still logged, so the record shows whether the tests sort winners from losers.
- Exit a drift position at the next open after the first session that closes below its open on heavy volume, or closes below the midpoint between the reaction day's previous close and its close, or after five sessions without a new high, and in any case after ten sessions. Never average up.
- Data that is missing (no consensus revenue figure, no average volume) makes that test "missing", and a stock with a missing test cannot be "enter". Do not guess a figure to complete a test.
- The record: every scanned reporter is settled five sessions after its reaction day against the S&P 500, and the script reports the share that beat the index and the average gap for each verdict (DRIFT SCAN block, and the weekly report). From pipeline version 7 (17 Oct) the settlement runs from the open of the session after the reaction day, the first price an order can get, to the close five sessions after the reaction day, with the index measured over the same span; the old figure from the reaction day's close is kept beside it. For a reporter that is not on the list, the run must give the full row (open, high, low, close, volume) for the session after the reaction day from its history page under `extra_history` (the DRIFT NEEDS line names them); from pipeline version 7.2 (18 Oct) a reporter whose next-session open never arrives is marked "n/a" after 15 sessions: its close-basis figure is kept for reference, but it counts as neither a win nor a loss, so the record holds only results an order could actually have had. After about 30 settled "enter" and "watch" cases, compare them with the "fail" cases before relying on the rule more heavily.

**Results ahead.** For any holding or watchlist stock reporting inside the next five trading days (and, from 17 Oct, any holding reporting inside the next ten, so that results clusters are seen in time):

- Fetch `https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/<ticker in lower case>-implied-move` for the move priced by the options market (the at-the-money straddle expiring just after the report) and its table of past implied versus actual moves. It is a third-party page: say where the figure came from, and say so if it is unavailable. Store the figure as `implied_pct` on that stock's results event.
- Note how the stock closed after its last several reports, whether moves tended to exceed the implied move, and the date and time, confirmed or estimated.
- Results risk budget: weight times implied move should not exceed 0.6 points of the portfolio (10% at a 6% implied move), **except for reports dated 27 to 30 Oct 2026, where it is 1.0 points** (15% at a 6% move, 20% at 5%), agreed by Tim on 19 Oct for the October results cluster. The reason: the entry is about 5 points behind the top 2% line, a results day is close to a fair bet, and removing every report's swing would remove the variance an entry behind the line needs. The pipeline applies the right budget to each report (version 7.3) and prints it; the page's Risk tab shows it. With no implied move, use the average absolute move of the last four reports, or 6%.
- The budget is a ceiling, not a target. A holding whose score has slipped below 50, or that carries a "downtrend" or "below 50-day average" flag, is still cut to 5% or exited before its report whatever the budget (the TSMC lesson, state doc section 10), and the RESULTS TOTAL is still quoted and judged.
- State the call for each: hold through and at what weight, trim before, or exit before, and the last order time that achieves it (before 07:00 UK on the day the order must fill, part C).
- Every holding's results date must be on the calendar as a results event, because the results total and the page read events only. On 18 Oct AbbVie (10%, 30 Oct) had none, so the total showed 5.40 points instead of 6.00. From pipeline version 7.3 the run prints `RESULTS MISSING` and a WARNING for a holding whose own next-results text names a date with no event: add the event that night (data doc G3).
- Results total (pipeline version 7). The RESULTS TOTAL lines add weight times implied move over every holding reporting inside the next five and ten sessions (a missing implied move counts at 6%). Reports in the same week tend to move together when they share a theme, so the per-report budget alone can hide the risk. Quote the totals in the review and say whether that much results exposure in one week is deliberate; when several holdings in one theme report in the same week, consider trimming the most correlated one first.
- After the report, the drift tests apply to the holding too: a holding that passes "enter" may be kept or raised within its limits; one that fails "reaction" and "held" is a candidate to exit at the next open.

**Analyst changes.** Weight the change (upgrades, target raises, estimate raises, especially several in a short period) far more than the size of the gap to the consensus target, which is a lagging 12-month view. Do not confuse one bank's target with the consensus. Log every change on a holding or watchlist stock in the `revisions` list.

**Watchlist.** Tim wants five to ten new candidates added every evening, not a weekly batch. The list means the stocks that are not held; the script's WATCHLIST line counts them. Keep it at about 20 and never above 25.

- Portfolio fit (19 Oct evening). A high score makes a stock a candidate, not a better holding. The run prints a PORTFOLIO FIT line for every strong watchlist name (G4): its theme's weight in the order now, its correlation with the order, and its highest correlation with any one holding. A candidate correlated 0.8 or more with a holding is a swap for that holding, never an addition. One that would take its theme above about 45% needs a holding of the same theme to go. When two candidates are close, prefer the one that adds less of what is already held, unless the review argues that more of the same is the deliberate bet (gate question 4). Diversification is not an aim in itself.
- Intake, every evening: five to ten stocks not already on the list, drawn first from the broad scan's Events and Leaders lists (data doc, G7; at least half when enough pass a first look), then from the earnings-drift scan ("enter" and "watch" verdicts first), the day's large-cap movers on the S&P 500 list page, the day's upgrades and target raises from major banks, stocks reporting in the next ten trading days with a supportive lead-in, year-to-date leaders, and two or three FTSE 100 names in the leading themes. US-listed or London-listed shares only, because part F has no price source for other European listings (a European company with a US-listed line can go in through that line). Large, well-known listings only (part C). Do not add a stock just to make up the number: if fewer than five pass a first look, add fewer and say so.
- Each new stock goes into today.json with full data (H1 step 4) so the pipeline scores it properly, with `added` set to today.
- Never remove a stock that left the order within the last five sessions: its prices feed the standstill test (below), and a stock off the list stops being priced. If one was removed anyway, the template names it and the run gives its row under `extra_history`.
- Removal, every evening. First: every ROTATE OUT candidate (weak, with no results inside five sessions) that has been on the list for three sessions or more, whatever the size of the list (`added` gives the date). Then, only if the list is still above 20: the lowest scores first, among middling names that have been listed for three sessions or more and have no dated catalyst. "Weakest" always means the lowest score. Never remove a stock with status "Results ahead" inside five sessions of its report, or one with a "Drift:" status inside ten sessions of its reaction day. Keep at least two steady names (a weight cap of 20% in the scores table, which marks a small daily range) and no more than six watchlist stocks in one theme.
- If one of a new candidate's pages is refused or missing, leave that candidate out tonight.
- Statuses (free text, kept short): "Watching", "Needs follow-through", "Follow-through confirmed", "Faded gap: wait", "Results ahead", "Drift: day 1", "Drift: needs 2nd day", "Ready", "Exit signal". The script sets "Holding" for anything in the order and turns a stock that leaves the order back to "Watching"; give it a better status if one applies.
- A position bought under the news momentum rule has a `note` that starts "Momentum position:", and one bought under the drift rule a `note` that starts "Drift position:", so later runs can tell.
- Every addition, removal and status change is set out in one table: stock, added or removed, score, band, reason. On a change evening the table goes in the message. Every evening the additions and removals also go on the page as one alert (H1 step 6), so Tim can see them without a message.
- A stock that leaves the list still has open shadow ratings. The script names such stocks in its SHADOW line; the weekly review settles them (H5).

**Crypto.** Tim wants Bitcoin and the other large coins tracked, so that stocks which move with them are judged with them. The script does the measuring; these are the rules.

- What is measured. Every evening the script prints a CRYPTO block: Bitcoin's and Ether's trend, read from the IBIT and ETHA funds (against their 20-day, 50-day and 200-day averages, with the change over 5 and 20 sessions), and for each stock the move that has gone with a 1% move in Bitcoin over the last 120 sessions, with the correlation and the same figure once the S&P 500's own move is allowed for. The trend is "up" when the fund is above both its 50-day and 200-day averages, "down" when it is below both, and "mixed" otherwise.
- A stock is crypto-linked if its theme is "Crypto", or if its daily moves have had a correlation of 0.5 or more with either fund over 60 or more sessions. The script decides this, sets `crypto_link`, and adds the flag "moves with Bitcoin, whose trend is ..." to the stock. Use the theme "Crypto" for a company whose business is crypto: a company holding coins on its balance sheet, an exchange or broker that lives on coin trading, a miner.
- Judge Bitcoin first. A crypto-linked stock is bought only when Bitcoin's trend is "up" and the IBIT fund is above its 20-day average, on top of the ordinary rules (a score of 60 or more with no "downtrend" or "below 50-day average" flag for a core position, or the news momentum or drift test). Good news on the company does not outweigh a falling Bitcoin: the measured link is the larger force.
- Size. Never above the stock's volatility cap, which is 5% for most of them, and never above 10%. All crypto-linked holdings together stay at 15% or less. Think of the position as geared: weight times the measured link is the portfolio's exposure to Bitcoin, so 5% in a stock that moves 1.8% for each 1% is like 9% of the portfolio in Bitcoin. The script prints a NOTE when either limit is broken.
- Exit. A crypto-linked holding is replaced at the next open when Bitcoin's trend turns "down" (the script prints a NOTE), or when the IBIT fund closes below its 50-day average, whatever the stock's own score.
- Timing. Coins trade round the clock and at weekends; game orders fill at the open. A weekend or overnight jump in Bitcoin is therefore already in the price the order gets, and is no reason to buy on Monday. What can be used is the trend. For a holding, a weekend move cannot be dodged (part C); what to judge is whether it is likely to carry on after the open.
- Live readings. The stored coin prices are those at the US close. When a morning check, a weekend check or a price refresh gives the script a live Bitcoin reading (`macro_now`), it prints the move since the stored close and, for every crypto-linked stock, the move that has gone with it, in percent and, for a holding, in points of the portfolio. That figure is the expected gap at the open.
- The trackers. Strategy and Coinbase are on the watchlist as standing trackers, at Tim's request. Their notes start "Crypto tracker:", the script leaves them out of its ROTATE OUT line, and they stay on the list whatever they score. They are bought only under the rules above. Other crypto-linked names (Robinhood, MARA Holdings, Riot Platforms, Galaxy Digital) may join through the ordinary intake when Bitcoin's trend is up; they are ordinary watchlist stocks and can be rotated out.
- The scenario "Bitcoin falls 15%" is kept in the scenario list. Its `Bitcoin` entry is not a theme: the script applies it to every crypto-linked holding through that holding's measured link and adds the theme moves on top, so the scenario's own "Crypto" theme entry stays at 0. The other scenarios carry an assumed move for the "Crypto" theme like any other theme.
- Ether, Solana and XRP are tracked for context: a week in which Bitcoin holds while the others fall hard is a sign of appetite for risk draining, worth a line in the market read. Only Bitcoin's trend enters the rules.

**Yields and oil (the macro backdrop).** Every evening the script stores the closing yields and oil prices and prints a MACRO block: each series with its change over 1, 5 and 20 sessions, a backdrop label, and how the portfolio and the S&P 500 have moved with oil and with the 10-year yield over the last 120 sessions.

- The backdrop label is mechanical: a 10-year yield up 10bp or more over five sessions is a headwind and down 10bp or more a tailwind; Brent up 5% or more over five sessions is a headwind for the market and down 5% or more a tailwind. Rising yields hurt shares, long-duration growth most. Rising oil helps energy holdings and hurts most other things through inflation.
- This portfolio is not the market. Check the sensitivity lines: an energy-heavy portfolio has been rising with oil and with yields while the S&P 500 falls with both. Say which way the day's macro moves cut for the portfolio, not only for the index.
- In the market read, weigh the two against each other in plain words: the day's main bullish news, the day's main bearish news, the macro backdrop, and which outweighs which. Good news on a day when the 10-year yield jumps 10bp deserves less trust than the same news on a day when yields fall.
- Also read the other 10-year yields. A gilt yield moving more than 10bp in a day, or the gap between Italian and German yields widening by more than 10bp in a day, is a UK or European stress signal worth a line. Report "n/a" as the script prints it; do not work a change out by hand.
- The commodity figures stored at 21:30 UK are that moment's readings, since those markets trade nearly round the clock. That is fine: the same time each evening keeps them comparable.

**The day call.** Tim wants a bull case, a bear case and a decision on whether the next session is more likely green or red.

- The script works out the chance that the next session closes up, for the S&P 500 and for the portfolio at today's weights, from the 40 past sessions (30 for the portfolio) most like today on eight measures: the S&P 500's move over one and five days, its distance from its 20-day average, its 10-day volatility, and the moves in the US 10-year yield and in oil over one and five days. It pulls the answer halfway back to the usual rate (about 55% of sessions rise) and prints the conditions that are true today with what followed them in the past, each tagged BULL, BEAR or NEUTRAL.
- The reviewer writes the two cases, each three or four sentences, from the day's news, the macro block and that evidence, and may move the figure by up to 5 points either way with a stated reason (`adjust` for the S&P 500, `port_adjust` for the portfolio; the one `reason` covers both). The morning check may move it by up to 5 more. The script enforces both limits. If a later run for the same session leaves `daycall` blank, the earlier cases and adjustment stand.
- The call is green at 50% or more and red below. Report it with its figure and the usual rate, for example "Green, 53% against a usual 55%". Between 45 and 55 say plainly that it is close to a coin flip.
- Honesty rule: the direction of a single day is close to unpredictable. From 16 Oct the call is a research record only: it never changes the order or the cash level, whatever its figure. It may be considered as an input to cash again only after it has beaten the bar of always saying green over at least 20 settled calls, and only with Tim's agreement.
- Every call is logged and settled against the next close. The weekly review reports the record against the bar of always saying green.

**Economic releases.** For every US release on the calendar (CPI, PPI, retail sales, jobs, GDP, PCE, Fed decisions) store the consensus forecast and the previous figure on its event as soon as they are known, and the actual figure and a few words on the surprise the evening it comes out. The script adds what the S&P 500 and the 10-year yield did that day. The run's EVENTS block lists the coming events with their exact names, marks past releases that still need an actual figure (NEEDS ACTUAL) and shows the reaction to recent ones (RELEASE). Friday's releases are stored by the Saturday review.

Scenarios exist for the releases most likely to move this portfolio (CPI at present). Before such a release, say what a hot and a cool figure would mean for the portfolio, using the scenario lines for size. For other releases a sentence is enough. After a release with a scenario, compare the portfolio's actual move that day with the scenario's figure: if it went the other way, or was more than twice the size, change the assumed moves. Remove a dated scenario the evening after its date and add one for the next dated risk. The template copies the stored scenarios, with their assumed moves, to `_scenarios`: to change the list, copy that into `scenarios`, edit it and give the whole list.

**The panel test (does the score predict anything?).** The earlier backtest ran on stocks that were on the list because they looked strong, so it could not be fair. The panel test replaces it as the main evidence on the score.

- The panel: 30 stocks fixed on 15 Oct 2026, every fifth stock from the 150 largest in the S&P 500 by market cap (positions 3, 8, 13 ... 148 in stockanalysis.com's list as read at 20:45 UK that evening), chosen without looking at any stock's performance. Members: Microsoft, Broadcom, AMD, Walmart, Costco, Procter & Gamble, Bank of America, Palo Alto Networks, Wells Fargo, Linde, Citigroup, Analog Devices, Abbott Laboratories, Pfizer, AT&T, ServiceNow, Danaher, Valero Energy, Western Digital, Newmont, McKesson, Corning, Adobe, Quanta Services, Constellation Energy, Synopsys, PNC Financial Services, Marsh & McLennan, Vertiv, Hewlett Packard Enterprise. The list is in `panel.json` and is never changed, except that a stock whose feed fails for good, or which has fewer than 400 sessions, is replaced by the next position down, with the swap written into the file's rule text.
- What is tested: the 48 price-only points of the score (all of the trend part, 40 points, plus the five-day relative strength, 8 points). The session and volume points (12) need daily highs, lows and volumes, which the panel does not store, and the catalyst and revision points (40) are judgement, so neither can be tested this way. Say so whenever the result is quoted.
- The measure, fixed before any panel data was seen: on every fifth session counted from 15 Oct 2026 (so no two windows of one stock overlap), rank the panel by points and by the next five sessions' return minus the S&P 500's, take the rank correlation across the panel, and average it over the dates. Verdict: "useful" if the average is +0.03 or more with a t-statistic of 2 or more; "the wrong way" if -0.03 or less with t of -2 or less; otherwise "no evidence". At least 8 dates and 10 stocks a date are needed. History (dates before 15 Oct) and live (from 15 Oct) are judged separately. The script also prints the top-third-minus-bottom-third gap, the same measure starting a day later (which leaves out the first night's gap that an order filling at the next open cannot catch), each part's own correlation, and the score bands.
- The history part has a known weakness: the panel was drawn from today's largest companies, and a company is partly large today because its shares have risen. That lifts the panel's average return, but it affects the ranking within the panel much less, which is what is measured. The live part has no such weakness but builds slowly: about six dates by the end of the game.
- First result, 15 Oct, on the 15 members loaded that evening, 48 dates: average rank correlation +0.028 (t +0.5), no evidence; one-year and two-month relative strength leaned positive (t +1.3 and +1.5), the five-day return part leaned negative (t -0.7).
- What it changes: nothing automatically. **The standstill test (pipeline version 7, agreed 17 Oct; corrected in version 7.1, 18 Oct).** Every change to the order is judged against not making it, with the game's own arithmetic and stored prices. The STANDSTILL block prints three kinds of row:

- Change: for each order change, the new order and the order it replaced, each held fixed for up to five sessions from the change's fill (the new one filling at the open), and the gap in points: what the decision added.
- Week: the actual estimate over each week against the order in force at the close before the week, held unchanged. From version 7.1 that baseline is the order before Monday's fill, so a weekend rebuild is judged too (version 7 used the order after it, which left every Monday change out).
- Inception: the first order (Fri 9 Oct), never changed, from its fill to the latest fully priced session, as entered (with its 25% cash) and fully invested (the same stocks scaled to 100%). This is the fairest single answer to "did changing the order help?", because it does not depend on where a window starts. Its stocks that have left the list are priced through `extra_history` (data doc G3).

Expected benefit (pipeline version 7.4, agreed 19 Oct). Every change of order states, before it fills, what it should add over its five sessions against keeping the order it replaces, in points, with the thesis and what would show it wrong (`order_expected`, data doc G3). The script locks the figure once the change fills and sets the change row's actual figure beside it (EXPECTED BENEFIT). Over the rest of the game this says whether the reviews' expectations have the right sign and size; it is evidence about the process, like the rows themselves, and changes no rule by itself. Changes before 19 Oct have no expectation and are never given one.

**The trade decision gate (proposed 19 Oct evening, approved by Tim with PR #4; in force from 21 Oct, playbook 6.12).** Finding an attractive stock is not a reason to change the order. "No change" is a full and acceptable outcome of every review. A change of order goes ahead only when the review can answer all four questions in a sentence or two each, and the answers go into `order_expected` (data doc G3) before the change fills, where the script locks them:

1. **Why change?** A specific, new piece of information since the order in force was set: a catalyst, a deterioration (an exit signal, a broken trend, a weakened stock before its report), a risk to cut, or a clearly better use of the weight. "Its score is higher" alone does not qualify.
2. **Why is the replacement better?** Against the holding it displaces, not in isolation: the next five sessions' case, the downside, trend quality, event risk (results, data), and the PORTFOLIO FIT line (G4): what it does to the theme weights, how correlated it is with what stays, and whether it is a swap for a stock carrying the same risk or an addition to it.
3. **Why not keep the current allocation?** The no-change case argued on its merits: what the order in force is expected to do over the same sessions, and what is lost by waiting a day for confirmation.
4. **What does it do for the top 2% target?** The gap to the line (part D) needs variance in a deliberate direction, not churn. Say whether the change raises or lowers the portfolio's exposure to its main thesis and its main risk, and why that is the right way to move from where the entry stands.

The standard is qualitative and stays so: no expected-return threshold, because none could be supported statistically on this game's evidence. `order_expected.pts` (7.4) is kept as the review's subjective estimate, written down so the STANDSTILL test can later show whether such estimates had the right sign. It is never a reason to trade, never a threshold, and never quoted to Tim as a forecast. A small or doubtful case is a reason to keep the order. The gate does not apply to exits that a rule requires (the momentum exit, the downtrend replacement, the results-budget trim, a crypto exit): those state the rule. It must not turn into a reason to sit still when a rule or a real opportunity says move: this is a short, rank-based game.

A row missing a price says "not priced" (or "priced to" a date) and names the stock; it is never filled with a guess. Use it as evidence about the process, not as a rule: a few weeks in one market regime prove little. The weekly review reports all three kinds.
