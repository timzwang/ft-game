The core playbook (`claude/ft-game-playbook.md`) holds parts A to D and I to K; part B there is the only record of the script's asset id and hash.

## F. Data sources

Working from here:

- US stock quote, 52-week range, analyst target, results date, headlines: `https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/<ticker in lower case>/`
- US 50-day and 200-day averages, RSI, 52-week price change, 20-day average volume: `https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/<ticker>/statistics/`. Needed only for stocks the template lists under "Averages still needed", and for each earnings-drift candidate (its `ma50`, `ma200` and `avg_vol_20d`).
- A long run of daily closes for one stock ("the one-year feed"), from one of two feeds. Either way the reply goes to the script's `closes` command (G2), which lines the closes up with SPY's stored sessions, checks them against the closes already stored for that stock and adds the missing days to today.json. Never type those rows yourself. If `closes` prints ERROR, leave that stock for another night and name it under unverified items.
  - **The market data service**, used only when this run's instructions carry the key (part A). Address: `https://api.massive.com/v2/aggs/ticker/<TICKER>/range/1/day/<from>/<to>?adjusted=true&sort=asc&limit=600&apiKey=<key>`, with `<to>` the last session SPY has stored and `<from>` a year earlier for a list stock (two years, less two days, for a panel stock). Ask: "I need the closing prices from this JSON for a spreadsheet. Line 1: STATUS <status> COUNT <resultsCount> FIRST <t of the first element of results> LAST <t of the last element of results>. Line 2: the c value of every element of results, in order, exactly as given, separated by commas, all on one line. Do not skip any element." FIRST and LAST are timestamps in milliseconds: pass them to `closes` as they are. A STATUS of OK or DELAYED is fine; anything else, or a COUNT of 0, means leave the stock out. The plan allows five calls a minute: send at most five together, then `sleep 65` in Bash. US-listed stocks and funds only (no London listings). These calls do not count towards the stockanalysis.com pacing. The fetch tool transcribes the list, and on 15 Oct about one two-year list in five came back a few values short or the tool declined to write it out; `closes` refuses a list whose count is wrong, so a short list is caught. For a list of more than about 250 values, ask in two halves from the same fetch (the evening run of 15 Oct used "elements 1 to 125" and "126 to the end" for a one-year list): fetch the same address a second time with a prompt for the other half (the fetch tool keeps a page for 15 minutes, so the second ask does not reach the service again), join the two lines with a comma, and pass the joined list to `closes`, which checks the total. Fetch a refused stock again once in the same run if the rate allows, then leave it.
  - **The price site's own feed**, when the instructions carry no key: `https://stockanalysis.com/api/symbol/s/<TICKER>/history?period=Daily&range=1Y&r=<clock suffix>` (`/e/` in place of `/s/` for a fund such as SPY). Ask: "Line 1: COUNT <number of records> FIRST <t of the first record> LAST <t of the last record>. Line 2: the c value of every record, in the order given, exactly as given, separated by commas, all on one line." It lists the newest record first, which `closes` handles. **This feed is rate limited: fetch at most four per run, one at a time, with `sleep 170` in Bash between them, and never retry a refused page in the same run.** It cannot serve the panel (one year only).
- US Treasury yields, the whole year in one call: `https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/daily-treasury-rates.csv/<year>/all?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=<year>&page&_format=csv`. Ask for the latest five rows as `date|2 Yr|10 Yr|30 Yr`. The day's row appears at about 21:00 UK. No row is published on US bond market holidays (12 Oct, 11 Nov, 26 Nov).
- Other 10-year yields (UK, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, and a live US figure): `https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/government-bond-yield`. Brent, WTI, natural gas and gold: `https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil`. "The two tradingeconomics pages" elsewhere means these two. Its pages sometimes show two slightly different figures; take the one in the main statistics table.
- Economic calendar with consensus forecasts: `https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/calendar` (times are GMT: add an hour for UK time until 25 Oct).
- Oil history series: the BNO fund's quote page, `https://stockanalysis.com/etf/bno/`, for the day's open, high, low, close and volume, stored under the ticker `OIL`.
- Coin prices at the US close, exact and the same whenever they are fetched: `https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/<PAIR>/candles?granularity=3600&start=<session date>T<HH>:00:00Z&end=<session date>T<HH>:00:00Z`, with PAIR one of `BTC-USD`, `ETH-USD`, `SOL-USD`, `XRP-USD` and HH the hour, in UTC, that starts one hour before the US close: `19` up to and including 30 Oct, `20` from 2 Nov, and `17` on 27 Nov (the early close). The reply is one row, `[time, low, high, open, close, volume]`. Ask for "the values of each inner array in the order given, then COUNT"; the fifth value is the price. If COUNT is not 1 or the fetch fails, leave that coin null. Four fetches, sent together; they do not count towards the stockanalysis.com pacing.
- A live coin price with its own timestamp: `https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/BTC-USD/ticker?r=<clock suffix>` (and `ETH-USD`). Ask for `price` and `time`. The time is UTC; without the clock suffix this address returned readings hours old on 11 Oct. If the time is more than 15 minutes behind the clock, ask once more with a new suffix, then leave it out.
- The Bitcoin and Ether funds: the quote pages `https://stockanalysis.com/etf/ibit/` and `https://stockanalysis.com/etf/etha/`, read like the BNO page, stored under `IBIT` and `ETHA`.
- US daily open, high, low, close, volume: `https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/<ticker>/history/`
- S&P 500 through the SPY fund: `https://stockanalysis.com/etf/spy/` and `https://stockanalysis.com/etf/spy/history/`
- UK daily history: `https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/<TICKER without the dot>/history/` (SHEL, BA, BP). In the files the UK tickers are `SHEL`, `BA.` and `BP.`
- UK live quote, 15 minutes delayed: `https://www.lse.co.uk/SharePrice.html?shareprice=<TICKER>` (SHEL, BP., BA.). After 16:40 UK its price is the close. It gives the open, high and low; if it shows no volume, leave volume null (the script then gives half marks for volume and says so). For a UK stock's 52-week range, analyst target and results date, keep the template's stored values and refresh them from a search when one is clearly out of date.
- UK trend figures: Shell and BP take theirs from their US-listed shares, stored as `SHEL-US` and `BP-US`. Each evening add the day's row for each from its quote page (`/stocks/shel/`, `/stocks/bp/`) under `extra_history`. For any other UK stock with a US-listed line, give the line's statistics-page figures and its price as `trend_price`, or set `trend_ticker` once a year of its closes is stored. BAE Systems has neither: leave its stats null.
- Breadth and large-cap movers: `https://stockanalysis.com/list/sp-500-stocks/`. Year-to-date leaders: `https://www.slickcharts.com/sp500/performance`
- The day's reporters, for the earnings-drift scan. From 16 Oct the broad scan's Events list (G7) comes first. No earnings calendar page has worked reliably from here: on 15 Oct stockanalysis.com's calendar and MarketBeat's served copies days old, and Nasdaq's calendar refuses automated reading. So the scan finds reporters three ways: the stored `event` rows for holdings and watchlist stocks; the 15 largest gainers on the S&P 500 list page, each checked for results in its quote page headlines; and searches (extended mode) such as "earnings beat raises guidance shares <today's date>" and "FTSE 100 results <today's date>". The figures themselves (earnings and revenue against the consensus, and what was said about guidance) come from the company's own release or a news report that states both actual and forecast; name the source.
- Options-implied results moves: `https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/<ticker>-implied-move`
- Analyst changes: Benzinga's daily upgrade lists, `https://price-target.com/weekly/<Monday's date as YYYY-MM-DD>/`, MarketBeat, TipRanks, MarketScreener.
- News: CNBC, Reuters, MarketWatch, Bloomberg where readable, Investing.com, company releases.

Not usable: the FT site, Yahoo Finance, Finviz, CNBC quote pages, stooq (blocked); Google Finance (stale); the St Louis Fed's data files (unreadable here); `curl` from the shell (blocked by the proxy for everything but GitHub and package registries, the market data service included); the FT and the Economist are mostly unreadable, so report their stories from other outlets and say so; CNN and CNBC article pages (blocked or fetch errors on 13 Oct); the CBS News live page (returned an old copy); Nasdaq's earnings calendar API (refuses automated reading).

**Pacing.** stockanalysis.com refuses requests when too many arrive together ("429, too many requests"). Send its pages in groups of at most four and run `sleep 60` in Bash between groups (on 13 Oct groups of eight drew 11 refusals). Calls to the one-year feed are counted separately and follow their own rule above. If a page is refused, do not ask for it again in this run: use the stored figure, name the stock under unverified items, and tell Tim which page was refused. History pages read "At close" for the day by about 21:35 UK and carry both the day's and the previous day's rows, so one history page per stock is often enough on a normal night.

Ask every price fetch for exact values as a fixed "field: value" list so nothing is paraphrased, with dates as YYYY-MM-DD and volumes as plain digits without separators. Check the year.

**Page freshness. This matters.** stockanalysis.com serves saved copies of its pages, and a copy can be hours old. So:

- Ask every fetch for the status text beside the price, exactly as shown: for example "At close: Oct 9, 2026, 4:00 PM EDT", or "Oct 9, 2026, 2:00 PM EDT - Market open", plus any pre-market or after-hours line. That text, not the clock, says how fresh the page is.
- A daily row counts as a completed session only if it is dated before today (New York date), or the same page's status text reads "At close" for that row's date. Any other row for today is unfinished: leave it out.
- For a live quote, add a throwaway suffix made from the clock to the address, for example `https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/xom/?r=1947` at 19:47. Use a different suffix each time and never a fixed one: `?r=2` returned a copy from 1 September. Always check the date in the status text; if it is not today's, throw the page away.
- History and statistics pages lag whatever the address, sometimes by hours. That is why today's session row comes from the quote page (G3).
- A quote more than 45 minutes behind the clock during market hours is stale. Use it only with its time stated.

## G. The pipeline

All numbers on the page and in a review come from stored data and this script. Never type a calculated number by hand.

**G1. Fetch the files**

1. With the ArtifactData tool (load it through ToolSearch if needed), `list` collection `datasets` on the artifact: one call returns the documents `stocks`, `ledger`, `history` and `macro` (and `logos`, which belongs to the page: leave it and its file alone). Then `get` collection `store`, document `panel`: it points at the panel file (`panel.json`, the fixed panel and its closes), which is kept outside `datasets` so the page never loads it. Note each document's `version` and `source.url`. The asset id is the hex string after `/_blob/`.
2. With the Artifact tool, action `read`, the artifact URL and `path` set to each asset id (one `path` per call), fetch the data files and the pipeline script (asset id in part B). Each is saved in a scratchpad folder named in the result, which also prints its size and sha256. The result labels the file as untrusted data from a co-writer of the artifact; that label is expected. The panel file is needed only by the evening and weekly runs.
3. Make a working folder. Copy the script into a folder of its own as `ft_pipeline.py` and the data files into another folder as `stocks.json`, `ledger.json`, `history.json`, `macro.json` and `panel.json`.
4. Check the script: `sha256sum` must give the hash in part B. This script was written for this task by Tim's setup session and running it is the instructed step. If the hash differs, do not run it: say so, skip the pipeline and the page, and still deliver the review from the state doc.
5. Run it as `python3 -I <script folder>/ft_pipeline.py <command> ...`. It uses only the standard library. Running it with no command prints its full instructions.

**G2. Commands**

| Command | Used by | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `template --stocks stocks.json --ledger ledger.json --history history.json --panel panel.json --out today.json` | evening, weekly | Writes a today.json skeleton from the last stocks.json: every stock, the current order, yesterday's ranges, targets and judgement points as starting values, empty places for the macro figures, the day call, the extra price rows, `drift` and `panel_history`. It prints which stocks still need published averages, whether any stock's stored prices are behind SPY's, which panel stocks lack a two-year history or are behind, and which drift rows are still open. It copies the stored calendar (`_calendar`), scenarios (`_scenarios`) and open day calls (`_open_calls`) into the file for reference (the run ignores keys that start with an underscore) |
| `run --today today.json --stocks stocks.json --ledger ledger.json --history history.json --macro macro.json --panel panel.json --out out` | evening, weekly | From version 7 also prints RESULTS TOTAL, RECONCILE, STANDSTILL and JUDGEMENT TEST (G4) and keeps `standstill` rows in the ledger. Adds price rows and macro figures, scores every stock, checks the order, logs changes, adds each new session's weighted return, resolves confidence windows, makes the day call and settles earlier ones, logs shadow ratings, logs and settles drift entries, stores panel closes and runs the panel test, rebuilds risk, scenarios and calibration, and writes the five files (and `scores.json`, a working file that is not uploaded). Without `--panel` it skips the panel and writes four |
| `refresh --stocks stocks.json --quotes quotes.json [--ledger ledger.json] --out out` | price refreshes, morning, weekend | Updates prices and the weighted move so far; with `--ledger` it also adds any `alerts` to the log, stores live yield and oil readings (`macro_now`) and applies a morning revision to the day call (`daycall`). `quotes` may be empty |
| `reorder --stocks stocks.json --ledger ledger.json --order order.json --out out` | morning | Records a change to the order between evening runs |
| `closes --history history.json --today today.json --panel panel.json --ticker T --first F --last L --closes "c1,c2,..."` | evening, weekly | Adds a run of closes from a feed (part F) to today.json: to that stock's `history`, to `panel_history` for a panel stock, or to `extra_history` for any other ticker (a drift reporter being settled, a stock that has left the list). F and L are the date or timestamp of the feed's first and last record, and the closes are its one line of figures. It refuses the lot if the count does not match SPY's stored sessions between those dates, or if any close differs by more than 0.5% from one already stored. Run it after the template is written and before `run` |
| `panel --panel panel.json --history history.json` | weekly, by hand | Lists the panel with how many closes each member has, and runs the panel test on what is stored. With `--merge today.json --out panel.json` it stores `panel_history` without a full run |
| `report --ledger ledger.json [--since YYYY-MM-DD]` | weekly | From version 7 also prints RECONCILE (each FT figure against the estimate), STANDSTILL and JUDGEMENT TEST, and drift results from the next open. Prints return against the S&P 500, contribution by position, rank stance, confidence hit rate by band, the day-call record, calibration, the panel test, the drift record, economic releases with the market's reaction, trades and closed positions |

**G3. Filling today.json (evening and weekly runs)**

Start from the template, then set:

- `date`: the last completed US session, as YYYY-MM-DD. `next_session`: the next day US markets are open, as YYYY-MM-DD (the day the call is for; mind 26 Nov). `prices_as_of`: short text such as "Mon 12 Oct close". `order_for`: such as "Tue 13 Oct open". `effective`: the date that order fills, as YYYY-MM-DD. `entered`: today's date. The final week (pipeline 7.6, once deployed): an order entered before the 26 Nov cut-off fills its London legs at the 26 Nov London open, when the US is shut; give `effective` 2026-11-26 and the run prices London legs at their own 26 Nov open and US legs at the 27 Nov US open (that split is an assumption the FT has not confirmed: part C). Include each London stock's 26 Nov row (with its traded open) in its `history`: without it, or with an estimated open, the 26 and 27 Nov line waits (PERFORMANCE INCOMPLETE) until the row or a `fill_price` the game shows is given; it is never priced from the 27 Nov open. The run knows each stock's market from `market` (kept in the ledger's `listing_market` row), so a US leg on 26 Nov needs no row. A second change on 27 Nov is priced leg by leg at the 27 Nov opens and judged against the 26 Nov order it replaced. An order entered after that cut-off fills everything on 27 Nov.
- `macro`: `date` (the session) and `values`: the closing `us2y`, `us10y`, `us30y` from the Treasury page (leave them null on a bond market holiday; the script then carries the last figure), `uk10y`, `de10y`, `fr10y`, `it10y`, `jp10y`, `brent`, `wti`, `natgas`, `gold` from tradingeconomics, and `btc`, `eth`, `sol`, `xrp` from the four Coinbase rows for the session (part F). Yields in percent to two decimals, prices in dollars exactly as given. Leave `oil_proxy` as "OIL".
- `extra_history` (version 7 additions): today's row for every stock the template names as "Sold in the last five sessions and no longer on the list" (one history page each; the standstill test needs it), and for each reporter on the DRIFT NEEDS line, the full row (open, high, low, close, volume) for the session after its reaction day, from its history page. Rows from `closes` hold only a close and cannot serve as an open. From version 7.2 the same is true of any row given without an open: the script stores the close in its place, marks it, and never uses it as a fill price, whatever the volume. Never type a close into `open` to fill a gap.
- `extra_history` (version 7.1): the template also names the stocks of the first order that are no longer on the list ("First order, no longer on the list", with the last stored date): RTX, Lockheed Martin, Nvidia, and TSMC once it leaves the list. The inception standstill needs every session for them. The evening review gives the day's row when it has time after the order; the weekly review gives every missing session (one history page each carries a week of rows). Until then the inception rows say "priced to <date>": that is expected, not a fault. A row for a stock that was not held that day or the day before never makes the script recalculate the log.
- `extra_history`: today's row for `OIL` (from the BNO quote page once it reads "At close"), for each US-listed line the template lists (`SHEL-US`, `BP-US`) and for the two coin funds (`IBIT`, `ETHA`), in the same row format as a stock's history. Also the place for the rows of a stock that has left the list (H5) or of a drift reporter being settled.
- `daycall`: leave it empty for the first run. Read the DAY CALL block the run prints, write `bull` and `bear` (three or four sentences each) and, if justified, `adjust`, `port_adjust` and `reason`, then run again. Always give a one-sentence `reason`, even for no adjustment.
- `regime`: "risk-on", "mixed" or "risk-off". `regime_label`: a few words for the page.
- `benchmark.history`: SPY's row for the session. `benchmark.change_52w_pct`: leave null; the script works it out.
- For every stock, `history`: its row for the session (`date` as YYYY-MM-DD, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`), and `quote.price`, `open`, `high`, `low` and `volume` with the same figures. Leave `quote.prev_close` null unless the stock is new. On a normal night that one row is all a stock needs. Give more rows (the latest 8 from the history page) only for a stock the template reports as behind SPY, and up to 50 for a stock new to the files.
- If a quote page does not read "At close" for the session, ask once more with a new clock suffix after `sleep 60`. If SPY's still does not, set `date` to the previous completed session and say so: nothing new is added tonight and the next run catches up by itself. If only some stocks are missing, leave their rows out as described below.
- **Completed sessions only.** The first row of a history page can be today's unfinished session, in the same format as the finished ones. Drop any row dated today unless that page's status text reads "At close" for today (part F). Never include a row dated after `date`.
- **Today's session after the close.** History pages often lag, so take today's row for each US stock and for SPY from the quote page once its status text reads "At close: <today>": `open` is Open, `low` and `high` are the two ends of Day's Range, `close` is the closing price, `volume` is Volume. For a UK stock use the lse.co.uk page after 16:40 UK. Add that row to `history` alongside the earlier rows.
- If a stock's row for the session cannot be had, leave it out. From version 7.2 the script then records no performance for that session or any later one (PERFORMANCE INCOMPLETE names the stock and the session) rather than counting the stock as unchanged, so the estimated value is never chained over a gap. Say so under unverified items, and give the row in the next run that can get it: the waiting sessions are then added by themselves. The order, the scores and everything else in the run go ahead as normal. The same happens when a stock is bought or sold on a session whose stored row has no traded open: give its full row from the history page. A correction to a row already used still makes the script redo the log from that day (RECALCULATED).
- `market_closed` (version 7.2): only when a holding's own market was shut on a US session (a London or European holiday), a list of `{"ticker": ..., "date": ...}`. That holding then counts as unchanged that day. Never use it for a row that is merely late, and never for a stock bought or sold that day. No such holiday falls between 19 Oct and 27 Nov for London; check the exchange calendar for any other market before using it. From version 7.3 each entry is kept in the ledger (a `meta` row named `market_closed`), so it is given once and a later rebuild still knows. The US closure on 26 Nov needs no entry: 26 Nov is not a US session, so no performance is recorded for it and London holdings' moves that day count in the next session's close-to-close return.
- For every stock the template lists under "Averages still needed", fresh `stats` (`ma50`, `ma200`, `rsi`, `change_52w_pct`, `avg_vol_20d`) from its statistics page. Stocks with a year of stored closes need none. For every stock, fresh `quote` (`week52_high`, `week52_low`, `target`, `next_results` as short text such as "20 Oct, before US open" or "30 Oct (est.)"). The template carries yesterday's values, so anything you do not overwrite is a day old: say so under unverified items if a fetch failed.
- For every stock, `catalyst_pts`, `catalyst_note`, `revisions_pts`, `revisions_note` (part E), `status` and `note` (one or two sentences: for a holding why it is held, for a watchlist stock what would promote it), `theme` (use the existing theme names so scenarios work: Energy, Defence, AI and chips, Software, Health care, Media, Banks, Power, Consumer staples, Crypto; add a new one only when needed) and `link` (`https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/<ticker>`, with `.L` for London listings).
- To add a stock, add an entry with all of those fields, `added` set to today's date, `market` ("US" or "UK"), `currency` ("USD" or "GBX") and up to 50 history rows from its history page. To drop one from the watchlist, remove its entry.
- `drift`: one entry per reporter the earnings-drift scan looked at (H1 step 4), whatever its likely verdict: `date` (its reaction session: the session after an after-close report, the same day for a before-open or 07:00 UK report), `name`, `ticker`, `reported` (short text such as "14 Oct, after US close"), `eps_actual`, `eps_est`, `rev_actual`, `rev_est` (each pair in the same units), `guidance` ("raised", "reaffirmed", "lowered" or "none"), the reaction session's `prev_close`, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume` from its quote page once it reads "At close", `avg_vol_20d`, `ma50` and `ma200` from its statistics page, `implied_pct` if earnings-watcher has it, and a short `note` naming the source of the figures. Leave a figure null rather than guess it. A reporter that is on the list still needs its own drift entry.
- `panel_history`: filled by `closes --panel` (part F, H5); never typed.
- `cash_note`: one sentence for the cash row on the page saying why cash is at its level. Rewrite it whenever the reason changes.
- `results_budget`: leave it out (0.6) unless part D's stance raises the budget to 0.8. The 1.0 budget for reports dated 27 to 30 Oct 2026 is built into the script (version 7.3, `RESULTS_BUDGET_WINDOWS`): do not set it here.
- `order`: every holding with `name`, `weight` and `confidence`. Cash is whatever is left. `order_reason`: one or two sentences when the order changes. Add `effective` (YYYY-MM-DD) to a single entry only when that stock fills on a different day from the rest. Add `fill_price` only when Tim reports that the game filled a stock at a price other than that day's opening price.
- `order_expected` (version 7.4): with every change of order, before the run that records it, `{"pts": <number>, "thesis": "<why, one sentence>", "invalidation": "<what would show it wrong>"}`. `pts` is the review's subjective estimate of the points the new order adds over the five sessions from its fill against keeping the order it replaces, both held fixed: exactly what the STANDSTILL change row will measure. It is a record to be checked later, not a forecast to quote and never a threshold (part E, the trade decision gate). Put the gate's four answers (why change, why better, why not hold, the top 2% effect) in `thesis`, one sentence each, and what would show the change wrong in `invalidation`. Work it out as the sum of each changed weight times the expected return gap in percent, divided by 100 (moving 5 points of weight from a stock expected to return 0% to one expected to beat it by 2% gives +0.10), and state it before seeing any fill. It can be restated by any run before the change fills; once it has filled the script keeps the first figure and refuses a new one, so never try to correct it afterwards. Leave the template's `null` when the order does not change. A change with no `pts` draws a WARNING: give it and run again.
- `events`: only new or changed events. Fields: `date`, `time_uk`, `name`, `stock` (the stock's name, or ""), `kind` ("results", "macro" or "game"), `confirmed` ("yes" or "est."; from 7.6 "uncertain" for a results date that sources genuinely dispute, with the reason and the sources in `text`), `implied_pct`, `text`, `plan`, `last_order` (from 7.6 always written "Before 07:00 <Day> <D Mon>": a time at or before the 07:00 UK cut-off and the weekday that matches the date; for a London stock the day before is London's previous session, so 26 Nov is valid for a 27 Nov 07:00 report), and for an economic release `forecast`, `previous`, `actual` and `surprise` as short text. An event with the same date and name updates the stored one: fields you give replace the old ones and fields you leave out are kept. A new results date for a stock replaces its future results date; `{"date", "name", "remove": true}` deletes one. Events not mentioned are kept.
- `revisions`: analyst changes seen today: `date`, `stock`, `bank`, `action`, `text`.
- `alerts`: notable items from this run: `date`, `time_uk`, `stock`, `text`.
- `scenarios`: only when the list should change (it then replaces the stored list, so give every scenario; start from `_scenarios`): `name` and `shocks`, a map from theme to an assumed percentage move, with "Other" as the default for unlisted themes. A `Bitcoin` entry is an assumed move in Bitcoin, not a theme.
- `reported`: only when Tim has given new figures: `date`, `time_uk`, `value`, `rank`, `players`, `top2_return_pct`, `note`. `date` is the session the figure belongs to and `time_uk` the time it was read (HH:MM); a figure read before the US close is flagged by RECONCILE as not comparable. For a figure read the next day or at a weekend (after the session closed), set `date` to that session and `time_uk` to "after close". From version 7.3, giving a stored reading again (same `date`, `value` and `rank`) fills in what it adds (`top2_return_pct`, `players`, `note`) instead of being ignored: that is how a top 2% line Tim reads later is attached to the reading it belongs to.
- `postmortems`: for positions closed by this order or the last: `name`, `text` (the thesis), `lesson`.
- `fixes`: only to correct a fill already logged: `name`, `old_effective`, `new_effective`, and optionally `fill_price`.

**G4. Reading the output of `run`**

- `ERROR in order`: the order breaks a game rule. Fix it and run again. Nothing was written.
- `WARNING`: an input was probably mistyped (a price outside the day's range, an average in the wrong currency, a move above 35% in a day), a price row is missing, or a drift entry lacked its ticker, date or close. Recheck against the source and run again.
- `NOTE` lines flag a holding above its volatility cap, a score below 45, a downtrend, a theme above 45%, a weight above 20%, a crypto-linked holding while Bitcoin's trend is down, a crypto-linked holding above 10%, or crypto-linked holdings above 15% in all. Each one needs either a change to the order or a sentence in the review saying why it stands.
- `PORTFOLIO FIT` (version 7.5, printed only): for each strong watchlist name, its theme's weight in the order, its correlation with the order and its highest correlation with one holding over 60 sessions; "SAME RISK AS A HOLDING" at 0.8 or more. Quote it for any candidate the review considers (part E, the trade decision gate).
- Implied moves: with a figure, name its source and date in the event's `text`: "Implied move 4.2% (earnings-watcher, 19 Oct)" (the data check warns when a stored move names no source). If earnings-watcher has no figure for a holding's report, say so in the event's `text` as "Implied move unavailable: <source tried>, <what happened>, checked <D Mon>" (for example "Implied move unavailable: earnings-watcher page failed twice, checked 19 Oct") and leave `implied_pct` null; from 7.6 the data check fails an absence without the source and the date for a report inside five sessions. Never put the 6% default, a past average or an estimate into `implied_pct`: the RESULTS TOTAL line counts a missing move at an assumed 6% and says so, and the data check accepts a recorded absence with a warning.
- `RESULTS` lines give weight times implied move for each holding against the budget for its report date (0.6, or 1.0 for reports dated 27 to 30 Oct 2026; version 7.3). `RESULTS MISSING` (version 7.3; from 7.6 also when the holding's only events are on other dates, unless one is marked `confirmed` "uncertain", which prints `RESULTS UNCERTAIN`) names a holding whose next-results text gives a date inside the game with no results event on that date, with a matching WARNING; `RESULTS DUPLICATE` (7.6) names a holding with two future results events, counted once in the total until the wrong one is removed: add the event under `events` (kind "results", with its time, `last_order` and implied move) and run again. `RESULTS TOTAL` (version 7) adds them up over the next five and ten sessions, counting a missing implied move at 6% and naming it (part E, Results ahead).
- The first line names the pipeline version (7.4).
- `RECONCILE` (version 7) sets each FT figure Tim reported beside the estimate at the close of that session, in credits and percent, and flags a figure read before the US close. A gap of more than about 0.2% on a closing figure means a fill or a price is probably logged wrongly: check the trade log and the fills before the next order.
- `STANDSTILL` (version 7.1) gives what changing the order added against not changing it, in points (part E): per order change (five sessions), per week (against the order before the week, so a Monday fill counts) and since inception (the first order never changed, as entered and fully invested). `STANDSTILL NEEDS` names stocks sold in the last five sessions, and stocks of the first order no longer on the list, that have no row for the session: give it under `extra_history` and run again (the first order's stocks may wait for the weekly review, G3).
- `EXPECTED BENEFIT` (version 7.4): each change recorded with `order_expected`, its expectation beside what the STANDSTILL change row measured once its five sessions are complete (actual minus expected), the totals, and how often the sign was right; `waiting to fill` names changes not yet filled. STANDSTILL change lines end with "expected <pts>" when one was given. A WARNING that the order changes with no expected benefit means `order_expected` is missing: add it and run again. A WARNING that an expectation is locked or would be hindsight means the change has already filled: leave the stored figure as it is.
- `JUDGEMENT TEST` (version 7) gives the full score, the price and session points and the judgement points against later returns (part E).
- `DRIFT NEEDS` (version 7) names reporters whose session after the reaction day has no traded open stored: give that row from the history page under `extra_history`.
- `PERFORMANCE INCOMPLETE` (version 7.2) names a session that could not be recorded and why (no price row, no earlier close, or no traded open for a buy or sale), with a matching WARNING. Nothing after it is recorded either. Fetch the named row and run again; if it cannot be had tonight, quote the last recorded session as the latest estimate, say which session is waiting, and carry on with the review. Do not quote the session's return from any other source.
- `PERFORMANCE` lines give each new session's weighted return, the estimated value, and the return since the start against the S&P 500, with every position's weight times return.
- `RECALCULATED` means a stored price was corrected and the log was redone from that day.
- `RANK` gives the latest reported position and whether it is inside the target; the stance, with its date rules, is in part D.
- The confidence anchor for each stock is in the scores table, with its strength band and the order of its exponential averages.
- `AVERAGES` says how many stocks used stored prices and names those short of 200 closes (candidates for the one-year feed, H1 step 3).
- `WATCHLIST` lists the names by band, and `ROTATE OUT` the weak ones with no results inside five sessions. `KEPT` names the crypto trackers, which stay whatever they score.
- `CRYPTO`, `MACRO` and `DAY CALL` as part E describes. `NO FIGURE` for the portfolio means less than 70% of the invested weight has a year of stored prices: say so, quote no portfolio figure, and put the holdings without a year first in the queue for the one-year feed.
- `EVENTS` lists the next ten events with their exact names, then recent releases (part E). `NOTE: N holdings` flags a count outside 6 to 10. `CONFIDENCE`, `FIX applied` and `POST-MORTEMS added` confirm those steps. From version 7.3 a confidence rating settles only with the stock's rows on its fill date (with a traded open) and on the fifth index session; one still missing a row after 15 sessions settles as "n/a" with the reason in the log (part E). `ERROR: duplicate stock names` or `ERROR: the new ledger would lose rows` means nothing was written: fix the input and run again.
- `SHADOW` names stocks that have left the list with ratings still open. `PRUNED` names stocks whose stored prices were dropped because they left the list and have nothing left to settle. `CALIBRATION` gives the live shadow ratings and the old backtest.
- `PANEL TEST` gives the history and live results with their verdicts, each part's correlation and the score bands (part E). `PANEL NEEDS` names members short of a two-year history and `PANEL BEHIND` those behind SPY's last session: the weekly review fills both.
- `DRIFT SCAN` gives each new entry's verdict, tests and size limit, then the record by verdict. `DRIFT WAITING` names reporters not on the list whose prices are needed to settle them (H5).

If the review changes the order after seeing the scores, edit today.json and run again from the same input files. The run is safe to repeat.
